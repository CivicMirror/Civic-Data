Civic-Data Schema Guide

A shared, human-reviewable spine of U.S. civic identity data — jurisdictions, offices, people, candidacies, and elections — jointly consumed by CivicPatch and CivicMirror.

This page explains the data model for new contributors: the six entity types, how they link together, the field conventions, and what stays out of the repo. Normative field definitions live in schemas/*.schema.json; design rationale in docs/ARCHITECTURE.md.

The model in one picture

Government data is modeled as a chain: a division (a place on the map) has a jurisdiction (its government), which contains organizations (bodies like a council), which define posts (seats that exist whether or not someone holds them). A membership links a person to an organization and post for a bounded time. Elections record the contests that fill posts, with winners pointing back at people.

Jurisdiction

A governing authority within a geographic division. Keyed by OCD Division ID plus a governance classification. Carries the official website, government form, and site intelligence (what CMS/vendor platform the jurisdiction publishes on) shared by both projects' scrapers.

requireddivision_idstringThe geographic OCD division governed
requiredclassificationstringgovernment, congressional-district, etc.
optionalgovernment_formstringe.g. mayor-council, council-manager, open town meeting
optionalwebsitestringOfficial site URL
optionalidentifiersarrayCrosswalks to external datasets (see identifier convention)
optionalsite_intelligenceobjectScraper intel: CMS/vendor platform, page patterns

Organization

Post

A position that exists independently of the person who holds it — a title plus a seat count, within an organization. The post is the stable join key: CivicPatch and CivicMirror join their datasets on the post (the seat), not on people or boundaries.

FieldTypeNotes
requiredidstringSlug like braintree-ma/councilor-at-large — the cross-project join key
requiredorganization_idstringLinks to the organization
requiredtitlestringFormal elected-office title, e.g. Councilor At-Large
requiredseatsintegerNumber of seats elected to this post
optionalidentifiersarrayExternal crosswalks
requiredsourcesarrayProvenance — seat counts must cite the charter/bylaw or official roster
Title discipline: posts track the formal elected office created by charter or statute — not internal board roles (Chair, Vice Chair, Clerk), which boards assign to themselves after the election and which never appear on a ballot.

Membership

A time-bounded relationship linking a person to an organization and, usually, a post. This is where officeholding lives — person files do not carry office relationships directly. Start/end dates, how_seated, and sources are the core of the record.

FieldTypeNotes
requiredidstringUnique slug for the membership
requiredperson_idstringLinks to the person
requiredorganization_idstringLinks to the organization
optionalpost_idstringLinks to the post (almost always present)
requiredrolestringThe formal office title — e.g. Select Board Member, Mayor. Same title discipline as posts: a board's chair is recorded here as Select Board Member, because the chairmanship is assigned by the board after the election, not won on the ballot.
optionalseatstringWhich seat of a multi-seat post this membership fills — e.g. District 1, Ward 3, or At-Large. A geographic/at-large designation and a sibling of role, not a job function: board-assigned titles like Chair or Clerk don't belong here either.
optionalstart / endstringISO dates; end is omitted while the person holds the office. Never inferred — only recorded when a source states it.
optionalhow_seatedstringe.g. elected, appointed — never inferred from a name appearing on a roster
requiredsourcesarrayCertified results or official roster for the seating event

Election & Contest

One file per election, containing one or more contests. Contests link a race to a post (office_id), list candidates, and record certified winners as person IDs. This is deliberately small: precinct-level rows and live results snapshots stay in CivicMirror; only the reviewable outcome linkage lives here.

FieldTypeNotes
requiredidstringe.g. braintree/2023-11-07/general
requiredname / datestringHuman name and ISO election date
requiredelection_typestringgeneral, primary, …
requiredstatusstringcertified once official
requiredcontestsarrayEach contest: office_id (→ post), candidate_ids, winners[] with person IDs, result_status
requiredsourcesarrayCertified results document

Conventions that hold the repo together

Identifiers

Every crosswalk entry is {scheme, identifier} (+ optional source). Internal IDs are OCD-style: ocd-jurisdiction/…, ocd-organization/<uuid>, ocd-person/<uuid>, ocd-division/…. Post IDs are human-readable slugs (braintree-ma/councilor-at-large) and serve as the cross-project join key. Organizations carry {scheme: civicmirror-office, identifier: <post-id>} to bind the org to its post.

Sources

Every entity requires sources[]; every entry has url, a note saying what the source establishes, and retrieved (ISO date). Rules of thumb: official sources only; cite the exact page or document for every fact; certified results over specimen ballots; never infer term ends, elected-vs-appointed status, or seat counts — verify against the charter, bylaws, or an official roster.

Layout

data/us/<state>/{jurisdictions,organizations,posts,memberships,people,elections}/, tiered into federal/, state-upper/, state-lower/, county/, municipal/ (people are further split per town). JSON Schemas for all seven entity types live in schemas/.

Workflow

PRs are the write path — bots and humans alike propose changes as pull requests, CI runs scripts/validate.py (schema + reference integrity + duplicate detection), and humans merge. Ambiguous field names are defined in docs/GLOSSARY.md; open modeling questions in docs/open_questions.md.

Worked example: one mayor, end to end

How Erin Venezia Joyce's mayoralty of Braintree is represented across five files:

  1. Jurisdiction ocd-jurisdiction/country:us/state:ma/place:braintree/government — the government of Braintree, inside ocd-division/country:us/state:ma/place:braintree.
  2. Organization ocd-organization/cd978ff2-… — “Office of the Mayor, Braintree”, in that jurisdiction.
  3. Post braintree/mayor — “Mayor of Braintree”, 1 seat, in that organization.
  4. Membership braintree-mayor-joyce — links her person record to the org + post, role Mayor, seat At-Large, started 2024-01-01, how_seated: elected, sourced to her OCPF filer profile.
  5. Election braintree/2023-11-07/general — the contest with office_id: braintree/mayor names her as the certified winner, closing the loop from ballot to officeholder.

Her person file holds her name, candidacies, identifiers, and verification — and nothing about the office itself. If she leaves office, only the membership gains an end date; the person, post, and organization are untouched.

What stays out

  • Raw election results — precinct rows and live snapshots churn during canvass and make unreviewable diffs. Only compact, certified contest outcomes live here.
  • Internal board titles — Chair, Vice Chair, Clerk are assigned by the board itself after the election and never appear on a ballot. They are recorded in neither post titles nor membership roles: a board chair's membership carries the formal office role (e.g. Select Board Member).
  • Personal contact data — filing addresses, personal phones, and personal emails never enter this repo. Public office contact only.
  • Single-project data — the moment a field is only needed by one project, it moves back into that project's repo. This is the shared spine, not a merged project.
